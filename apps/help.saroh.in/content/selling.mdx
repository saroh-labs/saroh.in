## Selling

**Sell** in the sidebar is your sales channels. A **store** holds a catalogue,
its customers and its orders — so if you sell in genuinely separate ways, they
can be separate stores.

Most businesses need exactly one. Start there.

## Products

Inside a store, **Products** is your catalogue.

A product carries a name, a price and a status. **Draft** products are not on
sale; **Published** ones are. Use the *Live* and *Draft* filters to see which is
which at a glance.

Switch to the **Grid** view when you are checking that the shop looks right — it
shows the pictures. Stay in **Table** when you are checking prices, because that
is the view where the numbers line up in a column and a wrong one stands out.

Categories group products for your customers. A product without one shows as
*Uncategorized*, which is fine and does not stop it selling.

## Orders

**Orders** is a queue, not an archive. It is sorted so the thing that has waited
longest is easiest to find.

Each row shows what state the order is in and **how long it has been waiting**.
That is the column that decides what to do next.

| Status                | Means                                 |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Pending**           | Placed. Nothing has happened yet.     |
| **Processing**        | You are working on it.                |
| **Shipped/Delivered** | On its way, or arrived.               |
| **Cancelled**         | Stopped.                              |

Payment is tracked separately, because "made" and "paid for" are different
questions. **Unpaid** and **Pending** are drawn in amber — money owed and people
waiting. Green means done.

The **Needs fulfilling** filter is the one to live in. **Unpaid** is the one to
check weekly.

Orders needing attention appear on Home, with the age and value of each, so you
can usually decide what to do before opening this screen at all.

## Customers in a store

A store keeps its own customer records — the people who have actually bought
there. These are related to, but not the same as, your CRM
[contacts](/customers): a contact is anyone who has been in touch, a store
customer is someone who has ordered.

## Taking payments

Accepting money online needs a payment provider connected, under
**Settings → Providers**. Until one is, you can still record orders and manage
fulfilment — you just cannot collect payment through Saroh.
