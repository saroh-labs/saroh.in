## Bookings and orders from your site

A publishable key only reads. To take a booking, an order or an enquiry
from your own website, pick one of three ways.

| Way                       | You need                     | Good for                                   |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Your server            | A server and a secret key    | Your own checkout, an app, a sync          |
| 2. A visitor token        | A server to make the token   | Booking and paying from your own pages     |
| 3. Saroh's hosted pages   | Nothing                      | A website with no server                   |

### 1. From your server, with the secret key

Your server calls `/v1` with the secret key, as for any other record.

- `POST /v1/bookings` books a service for a customer. Name them by
  `contactId`, or give `bookerEmail` (and a name and phone if you have
  them). The booking follows the business's own hours and diary, as one
  made in Saroh does.
- `POST /v1/orders` makes an order, left unpaid. Name the customer by
  `contactId`, or give `customer.email`. Taking the money stays with Saroh.

<Sample>{`curl ${API_URL}/v1/bookings \\
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_live_…" \\
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \\
  -H "Idempotency-Key: booking-7f3a" \\
  -d '{"serviceId":"…","startAt":"2026-12-01T10:00:00+05:30","bookerEmail":"asha@example.com","bookerName":"Asha"}'`}</Sample>

The key needs the right to make bookings or orders. See the
[reference](/api/reference) for every field.

### 2. From the browser, with a visitor token

Your server makes a short-lived visitor token for one visitor. Your page
then books, orders or sends an enquiry with it, straight from the browser.
The secret key never leaves your server.

**On your server**, when the visitor is ready to book or pay:

<Sample>{`curl ${API_URL}/v1/storefront-tokens \\
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_live_…" \\
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \\
  -d '{"publishableKeyId":"…","visitor":{"email":"asha@example.com"}}'`}</Sample>

- The secret key needs the storefront right (`storefront:mint`).
- `publishableKeyId` names one of the business's publishable keys. The
  token takes that key's allowed origins, so it works only from those
  websites.
- `visitor.email` is the visitor you know, for example because they signed
  in to your site. Bookings and orders made with the token are made under
  this email and no other. Leave it out and the token can only send
  enquiries.
- The answer has the `token` (`st_live_…`, shown once), when it expires
  (about 30 minutes) and its allowed origins.
- Each call makes a new token. Don't retry it with an `Idempotency-Key`;
  just ask for another.

**In the browser**, send the token as a Bearer token to
`/v1/storefront/write/…`:

<Sample>{`await fetch("${API_URL}/v1/storefront/write/holds", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: {
    Authorization: \`Bearer \${visitorToken}\`,
    "Content-Type": "application/json",
    "Idempotency-Key": crypto.randomUUID(),
  },
  body: JSON.stringify({
    serviceId: "…",
    startAt: "2026-12-01T10:00:00+05:30",
    name: "Asha",
  }),
});`}</Sample>

What the token can do:

| Call                               | Does                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `POST /write/bookings`             | Books a slot to pay at the visit, or one with nothing to pay              |
| `POST /write/holds`                | Holds a slot while the visitor pays online: the whole price, or the deposit (`"pay": "deposit"`) |
| `POST /write/payments`             | Starts paying for a hold. Gives what the business's own payment provider's checkout needs |
| `POST /write/holds/release`        | Lets a hold go early, if the visitor changes their mind                   |
| `POST /write/orders`               | Places an order, paid online or at handover, with its payment when online |
| `POST /write/forms/{formId}/submissions` | Sends an enquiry through one of the business's forms               |

A held booking comes back with a `holdToken`. It is given once: keep it
to start the payment or release the hold.

The business takes payment with its own provider. `POST /write/payments`
and an order paid online answer with the provider's name, its public key
and `clientParams`, which you pass to that provider's own checkout. If the
business can't take payment online, these calls answer `409` with
`payments_unavailable`: offer to pay at the visit, or on handover, instead.

If the body's `email` isn't the one the token was made for, the call gets
`403` with `visitor_email_mismatch`. Leave `email` out to use the token's.

When the token expires, calls get `401`. Ask your server for a new one.

### 3. Saroh's hosted pages

No server? Link to the business's own Saroh pages. Products, services,
posts and plans from the [storefront](/api/storefront) carry a `hostedUrl`:
the page for that item on the business's Saroh website, at its own domain
if it has one, else its saroh.app address. The visitor books, buys or
joins there, and pays there too.

`hostedUrl` is `null` when the business has no published website.
