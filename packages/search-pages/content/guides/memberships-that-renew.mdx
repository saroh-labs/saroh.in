## Keep the plans simple

Two or three plans are enough. More than that and people stall at the counter trying to choose.

| Plan | Who it suits |
|---|---|
| Monthly | New members trying you out |
| Three months | Members who've settled in |
| A year | Your regulars, with the best value |

Make the longer plans clearly better value, so committing feels worth it. The [gym membership plan calculator](/tools/gym-membership-plan-calculator) shows what each length costs and what a member saves.

## Write the rules down once

Before anyone joins, decide and write down:

- **The renewal day:** the day they joined, or the 1st of the month.
- **How they pay:** UPI, card or cash at the desk.
- **What happens if they're late:** how many days you allow before the membership stops.
- **Pauses and cancelling:** how much notice, and how often.

Put the same words on your price list, your site and the joining form. A rule nobody saw isn't a rule.

## Ask for renewal at the right time

A few days before the renewal day, message each member with the plan, the amount and a way to pay. It gives them time to pay before it's due, and nobody feels chased.

On the day, those who paid get a thank you. Those who didn't get a short note with the amount and the link again.

## When a payment doesn't come in

Most missed renewals are a busy week, not a goodbye. Handle them in steps:

1. **On the day:** a friendly note with the amount and the link.
2. **A few days later:** a second note, and say when the membership stops.
3. **On that day:** let it lapse, and tell them they're welcome back any time.

Don't keep a membership running for weeks without payment. It's harder to ask for two months than for one.

> Each plan renews on its day and makes that month's invoice, ready to send with a pay link. A renewal that isn't paid is flagged for you with a new pay link. See [how to set up a monthly plan](/help/set-up-a-monthly-plan).
