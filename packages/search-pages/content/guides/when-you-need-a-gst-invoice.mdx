## Start with one question

Are you registered for GST? Everything else follows from the answer. If you aren't sure whether you need to be, read [Do I need to register for GST?](/gst/do-i-need-gst).

## Which bill to give

| You are | You sell | You give |
|---|---|---|
| Not registered | Anything | A plain bill, with no GST on it |
| Registered | Something GST applies to | A tax invoice |
| Registered | Something exempt from GST | A bill of supply |

Some registered businesses use the composition scheme, and they give a bill of supply too. See [the composition scheme](/gst/composition-scheme) for who that suits.

One business can give more than one kind. A clinic, for example, may have exempt consultations and taxable products. [GST for doctors and clinics](/gst/doctor-and-clinic-services) explains that case.

## What changes on a tax invoice

A tax invoice carries your GSTIN, a number in order, and the tax shown line by line. [Tax invoice or bill of supply](/gst/tax-invoice-or-bill-of-supply) lists everything it must show, with the three kinds side by side.

## When the money comes first

Deposits and advances can change when the tax is due. Read [GST on advances and deposits](/gst/gst-on-advance-and-deposits) before you take money ahead of the service.

## Ask your CA

These pages explain the general rule. Your own case depends on your turnover, what you sell and where. Ask your CA before you change how you bill.

> Add your GSTIN once, and your invoices become tax invoices with the GSTIN, the HSN or SAC and the tax split. Mark a service GST-exempt and its invoices are bills of supply. See [how to add your GSTIN](/help/add-your-gstin).
