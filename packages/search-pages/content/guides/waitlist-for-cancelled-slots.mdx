## Who goes on the list

When someone asks for a time you don't have, offer to put them on your list. Note four things:

- Their name and number.
- The service they want, so you know how long it takes.
- The days and times that suit them.
- The date they asked.

A notebook or a note on your phone is enough. Keep it short: three to five names for each busy day.

## Who to offer first

Go in the order people asked. It's fair, and easy to explain if someone asks why they missed out.

The one exception is fit. A freed 30-minute slot can't take a 2-hour treatment, so offer it to the first person whose service fits the time.

## How long to hold a slot

The closer the slot, the less time you can give each person to answer.

| When the slot is | How long to wait for a reply |
|---|---|
| More than a day away | Two or three hours |
| Later today | Half an hour to an hour |
| In the next two hours | Message two or three people at once; the first to reply gets it |

Say the time limit in your message, so nobody is upset when it goes to the next person.

## What to send

Keep it short and easy to answer: “Hi Rahul, a 5 pm slot opened tomorrow for a haircut. Want it? Reply yes by 2 pm and it's yours.”

When they say yes, confirm the time straight away, then tell the others on the list that it's gone.

> Your booking page shows only real free times, from your team's hours, and your team can move a booking to another free time. See [how to set your team's hours](/help/set-your-teams-hours).

## Keep the list fresh

- Take people off once they're booked.
- Ask how long they're happy to wait, and take them off after that.
- For classes, it works the same way: when someone drops out, offer the place to the next person.
