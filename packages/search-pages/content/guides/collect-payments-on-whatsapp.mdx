## Ask clearly, once

A vague message gets a vague answer. Every request for money should say four things:

1. What it's for.
2. How much.
3. By when.
4. How to pay.

For example: “Hi Ravi, your cake for Saturday comes to ₹1,800. Please pay by Friday evening, by UPI to the QR below or the link. Thank you!”

Send it from your business number, so customers know it's really you.

## Two ways to take the money

| Way | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Your UPI ID or QR code | The customer opens their UPI app and pays you directly | Small amounts, regulars, payments at the counter |
| A pay link | The customer taps a link and pays by UPI or card | Larger amounts, new customers, anyone who prefers a card |

A UPI QR code is free and quick, but you match each payment by hand. A pay link comes from a payment provider, such as Razorpay or Cashfree. It costs a small fee per payment, and it tells you who paid for what.

Need a QR code to share or print? The [UPI QR code generator](/tools/upi-qr-code-generator) makes one.

## Check before you hand over

Wait until the money shows in your bank or UPI app, not just a screenshot. Then reply with a short thank you. A receipt or invoice with a number on it helps both of you if there's ever a question.

## When someone hasn't paid

Most late payers have simply forgotten. Keep the first follow-up light, and get firmer only if needed.

- **A day after it was due:** “Hi Ravi, a quick note that ₹1,800 for Saturday's cake is still open. Here's the link again.”
- **A few days later:** repeat the amount and the link, and ask when they can pay.
- **After that:** call them. A short, polite call settles more than another message.

Keep every follow-up on the same chat, with the amount and the link each time, so they never have to scroll back.
