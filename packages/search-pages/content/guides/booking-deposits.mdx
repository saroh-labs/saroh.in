## When a deposit helps

Ask for a deposit when a no-show would cost you more than the slot:

- **Long services:** if they don't come, you lose hours, not minutes.
- **Services you prepare for:** you mix colour, order supplies or block a room.
- **Your busiest times:** Saturdays, evenings and the wedding season, when you could have filled the slot twice.
- **New clients:** you haven't met them yet, and they haven't met you.

## When to skip it

Short services, regulars who always come and walk-ins don't need one. A deposit there adds a step for little gain, and a reminder the day before does the job.

## How much to ask

Keep it fair for the time you hold, and easy to say out loud. A quarter or half of the price covers most cases.

| Service | Deposit to ask |
|---|---|
| A haircut, a check-up, a single class | None. A reminder is enough |
| Hair colour, a facial, a long treatment | A quarter of the price |
| Bridal makeup, a set of sessions booked together | Half, or the full price |

## Say what happens to it

Before they pay, tell them three things:

1. It comes off the bill at the visit.
2. It's returned if they cancel before your cut-off, for example a day before.
3. It's kept if they cancel late or don't come.

Put the same words in your booking message and on your site, so nobody is surprised. For example: “To hold your 3-hour slot we take a quarter now. It comes off your bill, and you get it back if you cancel a day ahead.”

> You set the deposit on each service: nothing, a quarter, half or all of it. Cancelling in time means before the free cancellation time in your booking rules. See [how to take a deposit when they book](/help/take-a-deposit-when-they-book).

## Deposits and GST

If you're registered for GST, tax on a service is usually due when you receive the money, and a deposit counts. Ask your CA how to show it on your bills.
