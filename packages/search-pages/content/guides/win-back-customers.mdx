## Know who has stopped coming

First, know when each regular usually comes. Someone who visits every month and hasn't come in two is worth a message. Someone who comes twice a year isn't late yet.

A rough guide, to adjust to your own customers:

| Business | Usual gap between visits | Worth a message after |
|---|---|---|
| Salon, for a haircut | A month or so | Two months |
| Gym or studio | A few days | Two or three weeks |
| Shop, for regular buys | A week or two | A month |

Your own records are the best guide. Look at when your regulars came back in the past.

## What to say

Keep it personal, short and free of pressure. Use their name and what they usually come for.

- **Just checking in:** “Hi Anita, we haven't seen you in a while. Hope all's well! Your usual Saturday slot is open if you'd like it.”
- **Something new:** “We've started evening yoga at 7. Thought you might like it.”
- **A small offer, for a long gap:** “It's been a while! Come in before the 15th and your next class is on us.”

Don't make them feel guilty for not coming. The aim is to make coming back easy.

## Message only people who'd want to hear

Write only to people who've been your customers, and stop as soon as someone asks you to. One unwanted message can lose a customer for good.

## Find out why

When someone does reply, ask what kept them away. If several give the same reason, such as timings, prices or a staff change, that's worth fixing for everyone.
