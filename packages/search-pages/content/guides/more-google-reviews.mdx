## Ask at the happy moment

The best time to ask is when the customer is pleased and still with you, or just after:

- **Salons:** as they look in the mirror after a good cut, or in a thank-you message that evening.
- **Gyms and studios:** after a class they enjoyed, or when they reach a goal.
- **Shops:** when they pick up an order they love, or a day after it's delivered.

Asking a week later works less well. The good feeling has faded, and the message gets lost.

## Make it one tap

Every extra step loses people. Send your Google review link itself, not your Maps listing, so it opens straight on the stars.

| Where | How to ask |
|---|---|
| At the counter | A small card with a QR code that opens the review box |
| On WhatsApp | A short thank-you message with the review link |
| On the bill or receipt | One line with the link or QR code |

The [Google review QR code generator](/tools/review-qr-code-generator) makes a counter card from your review link, and explains where to find it.

## What to say

Keep it short and personal. In a message: “Thank you for coming in today, Meera! If you liked your cut, a Google review would help us a lot. It takes a minute.” Then the link.

On a card, fewer words: “Liked your visit? Tell Google.”

Then let it go. Ask once. If they don't leave one, don't ask again.

## Keep to the rules

Google can take reviews down when the rules aren't kept, so:

- Ask every customer, not only the ones you think are happy.
- Never offer a discount, a gift or money for a review.
- Don't write reviews for yourself, or ask friends and staff to.

Honest reviews, good and bad, are what new customers trust.
