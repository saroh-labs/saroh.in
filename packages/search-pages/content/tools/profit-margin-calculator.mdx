## What's the difference between margin and markup?

Both start from the same profit. Margin compares it with the price you sell at, and markup compares it with what the item cost you.

An item that costs ₹600 and sells for ₹1,000 makes ₹400. That is a 40% margin and a 66.7% markup.

## Should GST be part of my cost?

If you are registered for GST and claim back the GST you pay on what you buy, leave it out of the cost, because you get it back. If you can't claim it back, it is part of what the item costs you, so put it in.

Your CA can tell you which applies to you; check with them before you set your prices.

## Why is my margin lower than my markup?

Margin divides the profit by the price, and the price is always bigger than the cost when you make a profit. So the same profit is a smaller share of the price than of the cost.

## What margin should a small shop aim for?

There is no one right number. It depends on what you sell, how fast it sells and what your rent and salaries come to.

Work out the margin on your best sellers first, then use a break-even sum to see whether those margins cover your monthly costs.
